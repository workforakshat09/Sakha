# Sakha story scripts

Four stories, about 4 minutes of narration each (390–410 words at the voices' measured 125–137 words a minute), about 4:30 once the verse is read aloud. Adding 3–4 scenes each would bring them to a full 5 minutes. Each scene is one painting held on screen for about 12–18 seconds, with one keyword and the narration underneath.

- **Images:** "existing" means a painting we already have. "new" means one to generate in the ink style.
- **Young Krishna:** he appears quietly in a few scenes of every story, as the thread that ties them together.
- **Legends:** where tradition tells something as legend rather than recorded history, the narration says "the story goes".

## 1. Meera — "They called her mad"

**For when:** you fear what people think. **Verse:** Bhagavad Gita 12.19. **About 4:30 with the verse read aloud.**

| # | Keyword | Image | Narration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Merta | new: a desert town in Rajasthan at dawn, a little girl on a rooftop | Five hundred years ago, in the desert town of Merta in Rajasthan, a little girl named Meera was born into a royal family. |
| 02 | Giridhar | new: a wandering sadhu placing a small Krishna idol in a child's hands | The story goes that a wandering sadhu once placed a small idol of Krishna in her hands. From that day, she never let it go. |
| 03 | Mine | new: young Meera asleep beside the idol, moonlight | She talked to him. She sang to him. She told everyone that Krishna, Giridhar Gopal, the lifter of the mountain, was hers. |
| 04 | Chittor | existing: m01 (Chittor fort at night) | When she grew up, Meera was married into the royal house of Mewar, and brought to the great fort of Chittor. |
| 05 | Queen | new: Meera in bridal jewels, quiet, holding the idol under her veil | She became a princess of one of the proudest families in India. But she carried her little idol with her, everywhere. |
| 06 | Devotion | existing: m02 (Meera singing to the shrine, Krishna watching) | Every night she went to the temple. She sang, she danced, and she sat with wandering saints, as if no one else was watching. |
| 07 | Whispers | existing: m03 (whispering court) | But in a royal court, someone is always watching. The courtiers began to whisper. A queen, singing in public? Dancing with beggars and saints? |
| 08 | Shame | existing: m04 (family turning away) | Her own family felt ashamed. They told her to stop. To worship the family's goddess. To behave like a queen. |
| 09 | No | new: Meera standing alone before a stern council, calm, head high | Meera listened. And then, gently, she said no. My heart already belongs to someone, she said. I cannot give it twice. |
| 10 | Alone | new: an empty palace room, a widow's white cloth, a lamp | Then her husband died in battle. Meera was young, and now she was alone in a palace that did not want her. |
| 11 | बावरी | existing: m05 (corridor of pointing shadows) | Log kahe Meera bhai baawri. People say Meera has gone mad. She wrote those words herself, in one of her songs. |
| 12 | Poison | new: a silver cup offered on a tray, Meera's hands reaching for it | The story goes that one day a cup was sent to her, called holy water. Everyone knew it was poison. |
| 13 | Unafraid | new: Meera drinking from the cup, eyes closed, young Krishna faintly behind her | Meera offered it to Krishna first, as she offered everything. Then she drank it. And she lived. |
| 14 | Heard | existing: m06 (Meera crying, Krishna beside her) | Meera heard every word they said about her. It hurt. She was not made of stone. But she never stopped singing. |
| 15 | Anklets | existing: m07 (tying ankle bells) | Pag ghungroo baandh Meera naachi re. Meera tied bells on her ankles, and danced. |
| 16 | Anyway | existing: meera (dancing in the moonlit courtyard) | She danced in the courtyard, in front of everyone who had laughed at her. Not to prove them wrong. Just because her heart was full. |
| 17 | Road | new: Meera walking a dusty road with an ektara, pilgrims around her | In the end, she left the palace. She walked to Vrindavan, Krishna's own land, singing all the way. |
| 18 | Dwarka | new: a temple by the sea at Dwarka, lamps glowing | The story goes that she reached Dwarka, by the sea, and sang one last song in Krishna's temple. And she was never seen again. |
| 19 | Still sung | existing: m08 (people singing together today) | Five hundred years later, her songs are still sung in homes, temples and schools all over India. |
| 20 | Forgotten | existing: krishna (Krishna with flute) | And the people who whispered about her? No one remembers their names. |

**Verse, Bhagavad Gita 12.19.** "The one who takes blame and praise alike, who is quiet and content with what comes, whose mind is steady: that one is dear to me."

**For you.** Praise and blame are weather. They pass. What you love, and how you love it, stays.

## 2. Arjuna — "The bow falls"

**For when:** you feel stuck, frozen or lost. **Verse:** Bhagavad Gita 18.73. **About 4:30 with the verse read aloud.**

| # | Keyword | Image | Narration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Kurukshetra | new: two vast armies facing each other on an open plain at dawn | Long ago, on the field of Kurukshetra, two great armies stood facing each other. Cousins against cousins. Family against family. |
| 02 | Archer | new: Arjuna on the chariot, his great bow Gandiva raised, proud | On one side stood Arjuna, the greatest archer alive. Brave, skilled, and ready for this day for years. |
| 03 | Charioteer | new: Krishna at the reins, glancing back with a calm smile | And holding the reins of his chariot was his dearest friend, Krishna. |
| 04 | Between | new: the chariot driving out into the empty space between the armies | Arjuna said, Krishna, take my chariot into the middle. I want to see who I am fighting. |
| 05 | Faces | new: Arjuna's view: old Bhishma and teacher Drona among the enemy ranks | And there they were. His grandfather Bhishma, who had held him as a baby. His teacher Drona, who had taught him to shoot. Cousins. Friends. |
| 06 | Shaking | new: close-up of Arjuna's trembling hands, the bow slipping | His hands began to shake. His mouth went dry. The mighty bow Gandiva slipped from his fingers. |
| 07 | Down | existing: chariot (Arjuna slumped, Krishna turning) | Arjuna sat down in the chariot, in the middle of the battlefield. I will not fight, he said. I cannot. |
| 08 | Stuck | new: Arjuna with head in hands, the whole battlefield frozen around him | Every road in front of him looked wrong. Fight, and lose everyone he loved. Walk away, and abandon everything he stood for. |
| 09 | Smile | new: Krishna leaning close to Arjuna, a gentle, almost amused smile | The Gita says Krishna looked at his friend, and smiled. Not because the pain was small. Because he knew Arjuna was bigger than it. |
| 10 | Eternal | new: a single flame passing from one lamp to another in the dark | You grieve for those who should not be grieved for, he said. What is truly you was never born, and will never die. |
| 11 | Your part | new: a farmer sowing seeds at dawn, not looking back | You have a right to your actions, but never to their fruits. Do your part. Let go of the result. |
| 12 | Doubt | new: Arjuna listening, still uncertain, dark clouds behind him | Arjuna listened. He asked question after question. Still, his heart could not see what Krishna was seeing. |
| 13 | Show me | existing: deity (Krishna's face in the golden halo) | So he asked one thing. If I am worthy, show me who you really are. |
| 14 | Universe | existing: vishvarupa (the cosmic form) | And Krishna showed him. Suns and galaxies. Every life, every age, every ending and every beginning, all moving inside his friend. |
| 15 | Awe | new: tiny Arjuna on his knees beneath the cosmic form, hands folded | Arjuna trembled. This was the friend he had joked with, eaten with, argued with. He had never known how big he was. |
| 16 | Friend | existing: krishna (Krishna with flute, gentle) | Please, Arjuna whispered, become my friend again. And Krishna became Krishna again: gentle, smiling, holding the reins. |
| 17 | Clear | new: morning light breaking through the dust over the field | Something in Arjuna settled. The fear did not vanish. But it no longer decided for him. |
| 18 | Rise | new: Arjuna standing, lifting Gandiva again, calm and resolved | My confusion is gone, he said. My memory is back. I stand firm. I will do what must be done. |
| 19 | Stand | existing: hero (the empty golden halo) | The greatest archer alive had not needed a better bow. He had needed a friend who would not leave the chariot. |
| 20 | Yours | existing: diya (the still lamp) | And when you feel stuck in the middle of your own life, that friend has not left yours either. |

**Verse, Bhagavad Gita 18.73.** नष्टो मोहः स्मृतिर्लब्धा त्वत्प्रसादान्मयाच्युत । स्थितोऽस्मि गतसन्देहः करिष्ये वचनं तव ॥ "My confusion is gone, and my memory has returned by your grace. I stand firm, free of doubt. I will do as you say."

**For you.** You don't have to see the whole road. Take the next honest step, and let the result go.

**Images:** 14 new, 6 existing.

## 3. Hanuman — "Hanuman remembers"

**For when:** you've forgotten what you're capable of. **Verse:** Bhagavad Gita 6.5. **About 4:30 with the verse read aloud.**

| # | Keyword | Image | Narration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | Child | new: a baby monkey with a tiny golden halo, laughing on a mountain at dawn | Long before he was a hero, Hanuman was a child. The son of the wind. Full of mischief, and full of power he did not understand. |
| 02 | Sun | new: baby Hanuman leaping into the sky toward a huge golden sun | One morning he saw the rising sun and thought it was a ripe mango. So he jumped, all the way into the sky, to eat it. |
| 03 | Struck | new: Indra's thunderbolt striking the child's jaw, clouds swirling | Indra, king of the gods, struck him with a thunderbolt to stop him. It hit his jaw, his hanu. That is how he got his name. |
| 04 | Forget | new: wise sages raising their hands over the sleeping child | Many tellings say the sages, tired of his mischief, gave him one strange blessing: he would forget his strength, until someone reminded him. |
| 05 | Ordinary | new: young Hanuman serving quietly in a forest kingdom, carrying water | So Hanuman grew up gentle, loyal and humble. He served Sugriva, the monkey king. He never once thought of himself as mighty. |
| 06 | Rama | new: Hanuman bowing before Rama and Lakshmana in the forest | Then he met Rama, whose wife Sita had been stolen by the demon king Ravana. And Hanuman's heart gave itself to Rama completely. |
| 07 | Search | new: a band of monkeys and bears searching the southern coast | Search parties went out in every direction. Hanuman's group walked south, until there was no more land. Only the ocean. |
| 08 | Lanka | existing: hanuman (Hanuman on the cliff above the sea) | Somewhere across that endless water lay the island of Lanka, where Sita was held. A hundred yojanas away. |
| 09 | Impossible | new: monkeys sitting in despair on the shore, waves crashing | Who could cross that? One by one, the monkeys said how far they could jump. Not far enough. Not even close. |
| 10 | Silent | new: Hanuman sitting apart on a rock, quiet, eyes lowered | And Hanuman sat apart, saying nothing. It never even occurred to him that he could do it. |
| 11 | Jambavan | new: old Jambavan, the wise bear king, walking up to Hanuman | Then old Jambavan, the wise bear king, walked over and sat beside him. Why are you silent, Hanuman? he asked. |
| 12 | Remember | new: Jambavan pointing up to the sky, the memory of the sun leap behind | Don't you remember who you are? You leapt at the sun as a child. You are the son of the wind. This is what you were born for. |
| 13 | Spark | new: a faint gold light beginning to rise in Hanuman's chest | And something stirred in Hanuman. Not something new. Something that had been there all along, waiting to be remembered. |
| 14 | Grow | new: Hanuman growing huge against the sky, the mountain beneath him | He stood up. And he began to grow. Taller than the trees. Taller than the hills. His body shining like gold. |
| 15 | Mountain | new: Hanuman's foot pressing down on Mount Mahendra, rocks cracking | He climbed onto the mountain, and pressed down to jump. The whole mountain shook beneath his feet. |
| 16 | Leap | new: Hanuman soaring over the ocean, arms outstretched, clouds parting | And then he leapt. Over the waves, over the storms, over every voice that had said it couldn't be done. |
| 17 | Across | new: Hanuman landing lightly on the shores of Lanka at dusk | He landed on the shores of Lanka. And he went on to find Sita, and bring her Rama's ring, and his promise. |
| 18 | Already | existing: deity (Krishna's face in the halo) | Notice what changed. Hanuman didn't become stronger. He remembered that he already was. |
| 19 | Jambavan | existing: hero (the empty golden halo) | Sometimes we need a Jambavan, someone who sits beside us and reminds us who we are. |
| 20 | You | existing: diya (the still lamp) | So consider this your reminder. You have already crossed oceans you once thought you couldn't. |

**Verse, Bhagavad Gita 6.5.** उद्धरेदात्मनात्मानं नात्मानमवसादयेत् । आत्मैव ह्यात्मनो बन्धुरात्मैव रिपुरात्मनः ॥ "Lift yourself by your own self. Do not let yourself sink. You are your own friend, and you can be your own enemy."

**For you.** Write down one hard thing you once got through. That strength did not go anywhere.

**Images:** 17 new, 3 existing.

## 4. Prahlad — "The boy who would not bow"

**For when:** fear is louder than you. **Verse:** Bhagavad Gita 9.22. **About 4:30 with the verse read aloud.**

| # | Keyword | Image | Narration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | King | new: Hiranyakashipu on a dark throne, enormous and proud | There was once a demon king named Hiranyakashipu, so powerful that even the gods were afraid of him. |
| 02 | Boon | new: Brahma appearing to the king in a cloud of light | He had won a boon: no man or beast could kill him. Not by day or by night. Not indoors or outdoors. Not on earth or in the sky. Not with any weapon. |
| 03 | Worship me | new: the kingdom bowing low before giant statues of the king | He believed he could never die. So he ordered the whole world to stop praying to the gods, and to worship him instead. |
| 04 | Son | new: little Prahlad, calm and small, in a vast palace hall | But in his own palace lived a little boy who would not bow. His son, Prahlad. |
| 05 | Narayana | new: Prahlad at school, eyes closed, whispering a prayer as teachers glare | Prahlad loved Lord Vishnu, Narayana, with his whole heart. At school, instead of praising his father, he spoke only of Vishnu. |
| 06 | Rage | new: the king's furious face filling the frame, fire in his eyes | His father was furious. My own son, against me? Change his mind, he told the teachers. Or he will pay. |
| 07 | Calm | new: Prahlad sitting peacefully as teachers argue and shout around him | They tried everything. Lessons, threats, punishments. Prahlad stayed gentle and polite. And he did not change. |
| 08 | Snakes | new: snakes coiling around a calm, praying child, unable to bite | The Bhagavata Purana tells how the king tried to destroy him. Snakes were thrown on him. They did him no harm. |
| 09 | Elephants | new: war elephants charging, then stopping before the boy | Wild elephants were sent to trample him. They stopped, and would not touch him. |
| 10 | Cliff | new: Prahlad falling from a cliff, wrapped in a soft glow, landing safely | He was thrown from a cliff. Poisoned. Starved. And every time, Prahlad simply prayed, and was kept safe. |
| 11 | Holika | new: the king's sister Holika, smiling coldly, with a magic shawl | Then the king called his sister, Holika. The story goes that she had a shawl that fire could never burn. |
| 12 | Fire | existing: prahlad (Prahlad praying inside the ring of fire) | She sat in a great fire with Prahlad in her lap. But the flames turned on her instead. The boy sat in the middle of the fire, praying, untouched. |
| 13 | Holi | new: people throwing coloured powder at a bonfire at night | That is the night India still remembers every spring, with bonfires on the night before Holi. |
| 14 | Where? | new: the king grabbing Prahlad and pointing at a stone pillar | Now the king was beyond rage. Where is your Vishnu? he roared. Is he in this pillar? |
| 15 | Everywhere | new: Prahlad, small and fearless, looking up at his father | He is everywhere, father, said Prahlad. He is in that pillar too. |
| 16 | Pillar | new: the king's fist smashing the pillar, stone cracking with gold light | The king struck the pillar with all his strength. And the pillar split open. |
| 17 | Narasimha | new: Narasimha, half lion and half man, bursting from the pillar at twilight | Out came Vishnu as Narasimha, half man and half lion. Not man, not beast. At twilight, not day or night. On the threshold, not inside or outside. |
| 18 | Ended | new: Narasimha calm, the danger over, Prahlad bowing at his feet | Every condition of the boon was met. And the fear that had ruled the whole world was ended. |
| 19 | Mercy | existing: deity (Krishna's face in the halo) | Then Prahlad asked for one thing only. Not riches, not power. He asked that his father be forgiven. |
| 20 | Held | existing: diya (the still lamp) | Fear was louder than everyone in that kingdom. Except one small boy, who knew he was held. |

**Verse, Bhagavad Gita 9.22.** अनन्याश्चिन्तयन्तो मां ये जनाः पर्युपासते । तेषां नित्याभियुक्तानां योगक्षेमं वहाम्यहम् ॥ "Those who think of me alone, always devoted, I carry what they lack, and I keep what they have."

**For you.** Fear can be loud and still not be in charge. Name one fear today, and take one small step past it.

**Images:** 18 new, 2 existing.

Notes:
- **Sources:** Arjuna follows the Bhagavad Gita, chapters 1, 2, 11 and 18. Hanuman follows Valmiki's Ramayana (Kishkindha and Sundara Kanda); the curse to forget his strength comes from later tellings, and the script says so. Prahlad follows the Bhagavata Purana, Canto 7; the Holika episode is the popular tradition behind Holi. Meera's life mixes history with well-loved legends, and the legend parts are marked.
- **Review:** have someone who knows the tradition read these before launch.
